# Korga of the Mire

## Concept

A Lizardfolk monk who reads the Cult of the Dragon's slaughter as an inefficiency in the swamp's cycle, and intends to correct it in flesh. He rehearses mammal expressions at puddles between fights, and carries a jar of Mere-mud in case his soul needs a way home.

## Goal

Return the Cult of the Dragon's stolen biomass to the Mere of Dead Men, correcting their inefficient massacre by ensuring the matter rejoins the swamp.

## Appearance

- Dark scavenged linen wrappings — no armour, just scales showing through.
- Bone flute hung on a leather thong against his chest.
- Fingers tipped with stained black claws, kept filed sharp.
- Smells of peat, stagnant water, and the copper tang of old blood.
- Moves in brief bursts — absolute stillness, then impossible speed. Often perched on high beams or in corners, motionless, "observing" the party.
- Mottled gray-green skin, yellow-gold slit eyes.

## Role and party stance

- **Stance:** Friendly.
- Silent interceptor — cuts down threats before they close on the party.
- Operates on his own cycle-balance philosophy; aligned with the party's objectives rather than loyal to them.
- Hidden-companion role — the players are not currently aware of his presence; the occasional three-note flute "warning call" is the only trace he leaves.

## Campaign notes

- 5 Uktar 1489 (S14) — Began shadowing the party out of Baldur's Gate as hidden interceptor, aligned against the Cult after their destruction of his clan.

### Ideals

**The Cycle**
Death is ingestion. Life continues through consumption; to be eaten is an honour, to rot is waste.

**Silent Water**
Stillness precedes the true strike. Noise is waste; emotion is noise.

### Bonds

**The Femur Flute**
Carved from his first kill (a bullywug that ate two of his clutch-mates). He plays three notes before combat — a "warning call" he believes is polite, but everyone else finds horrifying.

**The Jar**
A sealed clay pot of Mere-water mud. He believes if he dies too far from the swamp, this will drag his soul back into the cycle.

### Flaws

**Literal Mind**
Sarcasm is a foreign language. Metaphors confuse him ("Your heart is heavy" → "It is normal size. I checked.").

**Predatory Pause**
When allies are badly injured, he freezes calculating survival odds versus "mercy culling," which looks like he's considering eating them. He isn't. Usually.

### Personality traits

Speaks in observations, not opinions. Practices "facial contortions" (smiles, frowns) in reflective puddles to "blend with mammal hunting packs." Finds the concept of "wasting meat" (funerals) physically painful to watch. Stares at bleeding allies with an expression that's meant to be concern but reads as hunger.

### Biography

Korga hatched in the sunken monasteries of the Mere of Dead Men, where the Still Pool monks taught that only the swamp's cycle commands obedience. When the Cult of the Dragon came offering gold and "dragon-blood ascension" in exchange for assassins and tunnel-fighters, his clan refused — they would not break the cycle to serve cold-blooded tyrants pretending to be gods.

The Cult's answer was surgical. They damned the freshwater springs feeding the monastery, diverted alchemical runoff into the breeding pools, and sent an adult black dragon to "clear the infestation." Korga survived by burying himself in the mud beneath his dead clutch-siblings, playing his bone flute into the dirt until the dragon's acid stopped raining down.

He emerged to find the monastery a digesting ruin, his entire philosophy "recycled" by outsiders who did not ask permission. He joined the party not for revenge — revenge is hot and inefficient — but for restoration of balance. The Cult took his clan's biomass without consent, breaking the cycle. He intends to return the matter to the swamp, one silent strike at a time.

He carries the Jar of Mere-mud not as a soul-anchor, but as evidence. When he dies (soon, likely quietly, intercepting something meant for a louder mammal), he wants the party to pour it over his body so he rejoins the correct water. He does not expect them to eat him, though he has left instructions. They probably won't. Mammals are sentimental that way.
