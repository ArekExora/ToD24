The gloss budget per artifact (set in [draft/SKILL.md](../skills/draft/SKILL.md) for `minutes-NN.md` and `gm-NN.md`, and [saga/SKILL.md](../skills/saga/SKILL.md) for `saga-NN.md`) distinguishes two operations that look superficially similar:

- **Rephrasing** — restating a fact already in the transcript in different (often more vivid) language without changing its information content. Permitted in `minutes-NN.md` and `saga-NN.md`. Example: transcript says *dawn* → rendering as *"the sky reddens as the sun crests the horizon"* is rephrasing — the underlying fact (it is dawn) is unchanged, only the wording differs.
- **Info-adding** — introducing in-fiction detail not present in the transcript. **Never** permitted in `minutes-NN.md` or `gm-NN.md`. Permitted in `saga-NN.md` only when the addition does not contradict, modify, or invent facts beyond what minutes establish. Examples: transcript silent on time → writing *"at dawn"* is info-adding (the minutes now assert it was dawn); transcript silent on weather → writing *"a cold wind cut across the keep"* is info-adding.

**Why:** these two operations are easy to conflate because info-adding *reads as* rephrasing — the prose flows, the added detail feels harmless, and the drafter's pull toward fluent prose smooths over the gap. The dawn failure in the session 6 retro was an info-add that propagated as canon: nothing in the transcript anchored the time of day, but the minutes asserted dawn, and every downstream beat (timing math, scene-end clock, character condition at re-entry) inherited an untraceable claim. The broader invention pattern is documented in [[feedback_no_invention]]; this memory is the specific test that lives inside the gloss budget.

**How to apply:**

- **The test.** Would removing the phrase leave a factual gap in the established record? If yes — the fact was already in the transcript, the phrase only renders it — it is rephrasing. If no — the fact only exists because of this prose — it is info-adding.
- **Atmospheric phrases are factual claims, not decoration.** *"The dawn comes thin and cold"*, *"gear smoking"*, *"the half-dragon dead-weight against his back"* each assert states of the world. Each must trace to the transcript via the per-bullet citation, or get cut.
- **`saga-NN.md` gloss is freer but bounded.** Gloss may be added as long as it respects the minutes' tale — no contradicting, no modifying, no inventing beyond what minutes establish.
- **Hedging is not a workaround.** *"Possibly at dawn"*, *"presumably the wind picked up"* — hedged invention is still invention. Unverified content goes to triage, not into the minutes as soft prose.
