## 1 - On the north lot
_~09:00?, 30 Nightal 1489 DR_

1. Breakfast at the tavern behind them, the party walks the north quarter to the wagon-lot where **Mia** has been standing watch overnight [Td:2-3]. As they close on the fence Mia steps out behind them from a rooftop she had been crouched on, in the hostiles' blind spot [Td:4-7].
2. She reports: in the whole lot the private guards never touched their wagon through the night — no cargo in or out, no visitors — though the lot itself is loud with other wagons being hitched for the north run [Td:8-11]. There are many private guards, not city watch [Td:9-11].
3. Sinsong lays a quiet working on himself to burn off the last of Elodin's compulsion [Td:14-16]. Eguzkilore sweeps the rooftops for watchers and catches only a hooded figure moving on business of its own, not following them [Td:18-24].

## 2 - The whistle swap

1. Nuberroca turns on Sinsong and asks after the magic whistle Sinsong had passed him months ago [Td:36-55]. Nuberroca reads his face [Td:56-60]: Sinsong is lying about the whistle, flatly [Td:58-66].
2. Nuberroca produces his own whistle and demands an explanation [Td:67-70]. Sinsong confesses — he had handed Nuberroca the wrong one by mistake and now offers to trade for the real one [Td:71-82].
3. Nuberroca will not take Sinsong's word for it. He puts both whistles in his mouth and blows them together [Td:93-94]. Nothing comes of it [Td:95-100]. A nearby guard turns at the noise and starts drifting toward them [Td:102-104].
4. Nuberroca looks hard at the two whistles and recognises them — they match the ones the city guards wear round their necks [Td:106-110]. He is not amused [Td:111-113].
5. He fixes Sinsong with the warning hand he had used the time he broke the stone, and lays down the rule — no magic from Sinsong on his person again, ever, unless it is healing. *"Como me eches algún tipo de magia que no sea para curarme... me voy a enfadar mucho"* [Td:112-123]. With an inspiration already on him, Nuberroca passes it across the bond to Sinsong — *"por estar huyendo de mí"* [Td:130-137]. When the dust settles, Nuberroca carries both the Silver Whistle (the real magic one, back where it belongs) and the Signal Whistle Sinsong had tried to pass off [Td:140-150].

## 3 - At the caravaneers' guild

1. The party talks the shape of it out on the lot. The Order of the Gauntlet has no local contact here; Carlon is dead and the Harpers in Waterdeep are not organised yet [Td:203-212]. The objective is unchanged — follow the stolen wagon north and track where the Cult of the Dragon is hauling its treasure [Td:250-258]. They will hire on as escorts to the next caravan, break off at any divergence [Td:300-305, Td:494-500].
2. They push into the caravaneers' guild. An old, half-balding manager works a desk buried under papers, running sums with an impressive dexterity, barely looking at the pages [Td:306-314]. He prices the work: five gold a decade for escorts, three a decade for construction, two gold bonus on completion. Eight to nine days up to the Carnath station, then two months on the road itself — forty-five gold a head if they come back whole [Td:316-337, Td:355-357, Td:425-437].
3. Sinsong tries to talk the man up to six. The manager asks the arguments; Sinsong offers that it costs him only six per decade and the party likes money, really [Td:386-410]. The pitch does not land [Td:542-544].
4. Nuberroca steps in with braggart's weight — *"Soy el famoso Nuberroca... somos los héroes de Greenest, rebautizados en los héroes de Mansken"* [Td:409-413]. He has Sinsong flash the revivify diamond; Sinsong, flustered, produces instead a small stone Eguzkilore had given him [Td:416-419]. The fee stays at five, with a one-off bonus for Nuberroca's visible strength if they sign [Td:462-466].
5. Kaelthar arrives at the counter and is caught up in a whisper [Td:471-487]. The party commits — six escorts on the next caravan, the one the stolen wagon rides with [Td:528-533, Td:633-644].
6. On the way, Nuberroca checks with the lot's valet-keeper about the wagon they came for. He claims Antón as a hired driver, their own man; the valet replies that Antón had described a different pair of men as the pickup, so he cannot release it to them [Td:570-600]. Next caravan to carry the wagon leaves at noon, about three hours off [Td:600-604, Td:616]. Nuberroca sets up where he can keep an eye on the fence [Td:618-620].

## 4 - A crate on the run

1. As the party signs the sheets inside the office, a private guard at the doorway holds the manager's attention with an abrupt stumble and loud bangs against the wall — a diversion [Td:647-651, Td:701-712]. Outside at the wagons another guard snatches a crate from one of them and runs with a second guard at his heels, carrying as if their lives rode on it [Td:714-726].
2. Nuberroca is after them in a stride, bellowing *"¡Al ladrón!"* [Td:726-728]. The surrounding guards converge on the sound, but it is uneven — several speed up toward the chase, and a few others, who had seemed worried earlier, visibly ease their pace [Td:770-780, Td:823-828]. Sinsong peels off to the party's wagon to guard what remains; Eguzkilore runs after Nuberroca [Td:782-786]. Nuberroca enters his rage [Td:788-793].
3. The thieves zig-zag through the parked wagons and Nuberroca loses the line — the lot is their ground, not his [Td:794-820]. He breaks off and scrambles onto a rooftop to look down over the maze [Td:829-837]. From the eaves he catches them crouched under a wagon with the crate [Td:838-841].
4. Nuberroca steps across the air in a cloud and drops onto them from above [Td:842-847]. Between shouts and lost bladders the thieves let the crate go [Td:849-855].
5. Nuberroca takes the one carrying the box by the arm and tells him in a quiet voice that if he returns the crate to its wagon and goes along quietly, nothing happens to him [Td:856-864]. The thief folds — his private guards do not pay enough to die for a crate [Td:859-861]. He walks the crate back under Nuberroca's shadow. To any curious guard, this was a misunderstanding — the crate had to be moved; it had the wrong wagon in mind [Td:869-872].
6. Watching the thief return the crate, the guards who had chased hard look at the ones who had slowed — some drop their eyes, some do not [Td:872-879]. One of the straight ones steps across and tells the thief *"no deberías coger las cosas que no son tuyas"* and walks on [Td:878-881]. Nuberroca pulls that guard aside: say nothing to Antón when he arrives — the party means to ride the next caravan behind the wagon, and the pickup should not know he is watched [Td:903-911].

## 5 - A sphere and a short rest

1. The party takes the hour on the lot, waiting for the noon caravan. They buy rations at seven silver a piece [Td:928-933, Td:995-997], refill waterskins and shuffle weight between packs [Td:998-1004, Td:1069-1088].
2. Sinsong spends his rest with the smooth sphere Mialee had lifted off Jorath in Waterdeep [Td:1057-1060]. He licks it and turns it in his hand — inside the glass a cloud of mist turns no matter how he moves the sphere, always pointing in the same direction [Td:1061-1067].
3. Sinsong hands it to Eguzkilore. *"Creo que esto lo vas a necesitar tú más que yo"* [Td:1067-1068].

## 6 - The caravan heads north

1. Noon of 30 Nightal — the last day of 1489 DR [Td:600-604, Td:616, Td:1198-1199, Td:1264]. Six wagons move out onto the High Road, the party among them — six escorts, twenty-four construction workers, all under the High Road Company's banner [Td:1198-1211, Td:354-357]. The three who had seen the Greenest cave-camp close faces on the drivers and workers as they come up; none triggers a memory [Td:1226-1236]. Waterdeep at their backs is drawing in for the turn of the year — braziers kindling in the squares, shutters half-closed, families focused on the long night ahead [GM]. The caravan's departure is quiet against it — a few farewell their men, most do not [Td:1237-1241].
2. The road is paved and patrolled; the travel runs to eight or nine days up to the Carnath station [Td:426, Td:1262, Td:1272-1275]. The caravan has no leader; thirty-six people walking the same road toward the same work [Td:1292-1310].
3. The first day passes without incident. Company barrels of food and water, no cook. The party camps for the night [Td:1309-1324].

## 7 - A hand in the pocket
_~10:00?, 1 Hammer 1490 DR_

1. Second morning on the road. Nuberroca takes a turn driving one of the wagons — his hand with horses is well above the ask and he puts flourishes on the harness, swerving the small bumps on the paved road while the actual driver dozes under his hood [Td:1326-1342, Td:1349-1355].
2. Mid-morning, Nuberroca feels a hand on his pocket [Td:1380-1385]. He checks his pack — a small object has appeared he does not recognise [Td:1389-1392].
3. He pinches it in his thumb and flicks it like a chip toward Sinsong's bald head further down the wagon [Td:1393-1395]. Sinsong catches it before it lands — *"¡Qué buen culo! Si quieres algo de mí, tendrás que reconquistarme"* [Td:1395-1398].
4. Sinsong confesses the game — he had tried to put a conjured spark on Nuberroca's weapon so he would have a lantern after dark. He touches the lance; it brightens in Nuberroca's hand with a steady light [Td:1435-1446]. The glow holds for an hour, and the sun is high, so the gift mostly amuses the rest of the caravan [Td:1450-1452].

## 8 - A horse by the road

1. Further down the road the caravan passes the carcass of a horse by the left side [Td:1469-1480]. Something very large has broken it [Td:1476-1479].
2. Eguzkilore reads the ground and the body. He draws it out — a heavy stone rolling down the mountain to the right has caught the horse and its rider, flung the horse through to the water's edge; the rock went into the sea with it. The horse was ridden at the moment, and the attack is recent, within a day or two [Td:1491-1504].
3. He speaks it to the group. Sinsong wants to press on and not lose the caravan's pace [Td:1537-1540]. Nuberroca defers to Eguzkilore — he feels the alert in his gut when he sees a thing like this, but will stay on the wagon if the ranger is calm [Td:1542-1552]. Eguzkilore chooses to investigate [Td:1553-1555]. Mia and Nyx stay with the wagons [Td:1561-1562].

## 9 - The orcs come down

2. An orc follows the boulder down the slope [Td:1607-1610]. Eguzkilore drops it with his bow before it reaches the road [Td:1611-1615].
3. Two ogres follow with giant javelins that look more like young pines than throwing-weapons [Td:1641-1644]. The first lays a javelin into the wagon's wood — the shaft bites but Nuberroca holds — then roars and plants a second in him across the next breath [Td:1649-1660]. Nuberroca drops the reins and climbs down to meet them.
4. Sinsong puts his back behind a wagon and reaches across the road with a word of compulsion aimed at both ogres at once — one shakes it off, the other turns and begins to retreat under Sinsong's *"huir, insensatos"* [Td:1957-1966].
5. ***[EPIC]*** Eguzkilore crouches to his waist behind a horse, strings the bow, and lets fly with everything he has in the time he has — four arrows one after another, closing on the near ogre like a hail. The creature is left bristling with shafts and limping, barely upright [Td:1935-1950 ?ord].
6. ***[EPIC]*** Nuberroca brings the halberd down twice across the same arc — the first attacker at his flank drops to the first swing, and his own god's dark fury carries into the follow-through to take the next [Td:1980-1996]. As he swings, dark-light tendrils climb his veins and the wounds across his chest close under his skin. *"De nada por creer en Sinsong"* [Td:2005-2012].
7. The distant ogre throws javelins back. The first takes Nuberroca deep across the chest and the second lands close behind [Td:2155-2165]. He is heavily wounded now.
8. Nuberroca sets down the halberd, picks up a crossbow and tries to put a bolt into the ogre at range. The first bolt catches it for a sliver; the second fumbles cold [Td:2173-2237].
9. ***[EPIC]*** Kaelthar closes the ground on his wolf, meets the last ogre and brings his javelin down with a dragon's roar — the strike opens the creature's chest like a flower, smoke rising from the dragonborn's nostrils as the ogre falls [Td:2309-2331].
10. On the other side of the wagons the guards' crossbow volleys have cut most of the oncoming orcs down before they reached the road; two guards leap on the last crawling ogre and finish him. Three guards wounded lightly, one badly, no deaths. Thirty silver among the orcs and swords too crude to keep [Td:2322-2348].

## 10 - False gods

1. The party takes the hour by the road to catch their breath. Sinsong tells Nuberroca across the camp that this time he will not heal him [Td:2369-2370]. Nuberroca steps across, taps his own chest, and the dark-light climbs his veins again — his wounds close under his own hand [Td:2372-2374].
2. Sinsong lets it go. *"No quieres que te desalbe, no quieres que te cure, no quieres que te resucite"* [Td:2374-2376]. Nuberroca does not soften it — *"No quiero falsos dioses, y si no me vas a resucitar, devuélveme lo que es mío"* [Td:2376-2378].
3. He names the cowardice as he saw it — Sinsong hid behind the wolf while two ogres came at him [Td:2378-2381]. Sinsong insists he handled both ogres with the word of compulsion [Td:2380-2381]. Nuberroca will not grant it — the second ogre could not have reached him under any circumstances; the compulsion only tipped the one that was already out of range [Td:2381-2385]. The quarrel ends with the caravan rolling on, noon of the second day, the next ten unaccounted for [Td:2397-2404].
